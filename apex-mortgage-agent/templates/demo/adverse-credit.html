What is adverse credit?

Adverse credit (also called bad credit or poor credit) refers to a history of financial difficulties that appear on your credit report. This includes missed payments, county court judgements (CCJs), defaults, Individual Voluntary Arrangements (IVAs), bankruptcy, and debt management plans.

High street lenders — banks like Halifax, Barclays, and Nationwide — use automated systems that reject applicants who fall below a credit score threshold. If you have any adverse credit, you're likely to be declined before a human even reviews your application.

That's where we come in. We work exclusively with specialist lenders who assess your full situation, not just a score. They understand that life happens — and they're willing to lend to people the high street won't touch, often at rates that might surprise you.

Understanding your credit issues

Each type of adverse credit is different. Here's what lenders look at and how we help.

⚖️ CCJs — County Court Judgements

A County Court Judgement is issued by a court when you fail to repay a debt. It stays on your credit file for 6 years from the date it was registered.

Lenders consider: the size of the CCJ, how old it is, whether it has been satisfied (paid), and how many CCJs you have.

How we help: Several of our specialist lenders will consider CCJs satisfied within the last 12–24 months, and some will consider unsatisfied CCJs depending on the value and date. We'll match you to the right lender for your specific CCJ history.

📋 Defaults

A default is recorded when you miss payments on a credit account and the lender closes it. Like CCJs, defaults stay on your file for 6 years.

Lenders look at: the value of the default, whether it's been satisfied, how recent it is, and whether it was on a mortgage or unsecured credit.

How we help: Many of our lenders will accept defaults registered more than 12–24 months ago, and some will accept more recent defaults for the right applicants. We'll find the best route for you.

📂 IVA & Bankruptcy

An Individual Voluntary Arrangement (IVA) is a formal agreement with creditors; bankruptcy is a more severe insolvency process. Both have significant but not permanent impacts on your ability to get a mortgage.

  • After 1 year of discharge: Very limited options, high deposit required (25–35%+)
  • After 3 years of discharge: More specialist lenders available, rates improve
  • After 6 years of discharge: Record removed from file, near-normal market access

How we help: We know exactly which lenders will consider your application at each stage. We'll be honest about what's achievable and help you build a plan if the timing isn't right yet.

⏱️ Missed Payments

Late or missed payments on credit cards, loans, utilities, or mobile contracts all affect your credit score. Even one or two missed payments can trigger an automated rejection at many high street lenders.

Lenders consider: how many missed payments, how recent they were, and whether they were on secured or unsecured debt.

How we help: Our specialist lenders are much more understanding about the occasional missed payment — especially if it was caused by a change in circumstances. We present your case in its best light and find lenders who match your profile.

🤝 Debt Management Plans

A Debt Management Plan (DMP) is an informal arrangement where you make reduced payments to creditors through a third party. DMPs are not legally binding but do appear on your credit file and affect lenders' decisions.

Being in an active DMP doesn't automatically rule out a mortgage — it depends on the lender and your overall financial picture.

How we help: We work with lenders who will consider applicants currently in or recently completed a DMP. We'll assess whether now is the right time to apply or whether it makes sense to wait until the plan is complete.

Why specialist lenders are different

High street banks run your application through automated credit scoring. If your score falls below their threshold — for any reason — you're rejected. No human review. No context. Just a no.

Specialist lenders take a different approach. They employ underwriters who review your application in full. They consider:

  • The reason for your credit issues (job loss, illness, relationship breakdown)
  • The trend in your finances — are things improving?
  • Your income stability and ability to afford the mortgage today
  • Your deposit size — more equity means less risk for them
  • Your overall track record, not just the bad moments

This human-led approach means many clients who were rejected by their bank are successfully mortgaged through our lender panel.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a mortgage with a CCJ?

Yes — in many cases. While high street lenders will typically decline you automatically, our specialist lender panel includes lenders who consider CCJs depending on their age, value, and whether they've been satisfied. Many of our clients with CCJs have successfully obtained a mortgage. Submit your details and we'll assess your specific situation.

How long does a default stay on my credit file?

Defaults are recorded on your credit file for 6 years from the date the default was registered — not from when you pay it off. After 6 years, it drops off your file automatically. Specialist lenders can often work with defaults less than 6 years old; the exact options depend on the value and how long ago it was registered.

Can I remortgage with adverse credit?

Yes. Remortgaging with adverse credit follows a similar process to purchasing. The key factors are your equity in the property (LTV ratio), your current income, and the nature of your adverse credit. In some cases, remortgaging can actually be easier than a purchase because you already own the property. We handle a significant number of adverse credit remortgages each month.

Will adverse credit always mean a higher interest rate?

In most cases, yes — specialist lenders charge a small premium to reflect the additional risk. However, the difference is often smaller than people expect, and it's usually time-limited: once your credit file clears, you can remortgage onto a mainstream rate. We'll always show you the full cost, including any fees and the total cost over the deal period, so you can make an informed decision.

Do I need a bigger deposit if I have bad credit?

Generally, yes. A larger deposit (lower LTV) reduces the lender's risk, which opens up more options and can improve the rate available. For mild adverse credit, a 10–15% deposit may be sufficient. For more serious adverse credit (IVA, bankruptcy, multiple CCJs), lenders typically require 20–30%+. We'll give you an honest picture of what deposit you'll need based on your specific situation.

How do I know which lenders will consider my application?

That's exactly what we do. Every lender on our specialist panel has different criteria — some will accept CCJs under a certain value, others require defaults to be over 2 years old, and so on. We know each lender's criteria in detail. Once you share your situation with us, we can quickly identify who is likely to lend, what rate you might expect, and what the next steps look like — all without leaving a footprint on your credit file.
